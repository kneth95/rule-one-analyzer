How Rule #1 works, and how to read this dashboard

Tap or hover any ⓘ for a plain-English explanation of a term.

1. The idea

Rule #1 means buying wonderful businesses at a big discount to what they're worth, so you're unlikely to lose money. A business is "wonderful" when it passes the 4 Ms.

2. What you decide, and what the tool checks

You decide Meaning, Moat and Management when you put a company on your watchlist. Write your reasoning in the notes on each stock page.

The tool checks the numbers: the Big Five, the debt, and the price compared with the MOS price.

3. The Big Five

Five numbers should each be at least 10% over the last 10, 5, 3 and 1 years:

  • ROIC: profit earned on the money invested in the business.
  • Sales growth: revenue growth per year.
  • EPS growth: profit-per-share growth per year.
  • Equity growth: growth of what shareholders own, per share.
  • Free cash flow growth: growth of the spare cash the business produces.

Growth numbers use CAGR. The Big Five score counts how many pass.

4. What the business is worth

  1. Pick a growth rate: the lower of past equity growth and the analysts' estimate.
  2. Grow today's EPS for 10 years to get Future EPS.
  3. Multiply by a conservative Future P/E to get the Future price.
  4. Discount it back 10 years at the 15% MARR. That's the Sticker Price.
  5. Halve it for a Margin of Safety. That's the MOS price: the buy price.

Two simpler checks confirm the result: the Ten Cap price and the Payback Time price.

5. Status and email alerts

  • 🟢 Buy zone: at or below the MOS price, with at least 4 of the Big Five passing.
  • 🟡 Getting close: within 10% above the MOS price.
  • ⚪ Not yet: everything else.

The analysis runs every weekday after the US market closes. You get one email when a stock moves into a better status, not every day it stays there.

6. Reading a stock page

  • Big Five table: ✓ and ✗ for each window. Hover a cell to see which years were used.
  • Valuation, step by step: every number with the formula that produced it.
  • Data warnings: where the data was thin or unusual. Read them before trusting a number.
  • Use this data: copy the yearly numbers into a spreadsheet, or a summary into an AI chat or your notes.

7. Using Discover

Every Saturday the tool runs the same analysis on all S&P 500 companies. The Discover tab shows three lists:

  • 🟢 Buy zone and 🟡 Getting close: companies you don't follow yet that already meet the alert rules. These are often empty, and that's normal: Rule #1 bargains are rare.
  • ⭐ Wonderful companies to watch: strong businesses still above their MOS price, closest first.

Open a company to see its full analysis. If you understand the business, click + Watch to add it to your watchlist: from then on it's analyzed daily and you'll get an email when it reaches a better status. Discover itself never emails you.

8. Limits

Numbers come from company filings with the SEC and from Yahoo Finance. Filings vary between companies, so check the warnings. Rule #1 also uses judgement the tool can't do for you. This is a research tool, not financial advice.
